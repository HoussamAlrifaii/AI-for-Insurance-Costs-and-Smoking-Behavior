MedCost Explorer: data, models and uncertainty¶

tl;dr¶

This notebook evaluates an educational model of historical medical charges. The source period and representativeness are not verified. Outputs below document the actual experiment; they do not validate current insurance pricing or clinical use.

Context & Methods¶

The primary task is predicting charges from age, sex, BMI, children, smoking status and region. Smoking status is an input, not an inferred diagnosis. Exact duplicates are removed before partitioning; missing BMI is imputed within training folds. Six fixed candidates are compared using five-fold CV on 60% of records; 20% calibrates prediction ranges and 20% is held out for final evaluation.

Key Assumptions¶

Random partitions assume observations are sufficiently independent and similarly distributed. Person identifiers and dates are absent, so this cannot be checked fully. Interval coverage relies on exchangeability, not merely a nominal confidence label. Descriptive EDA below uses the full dataset and does not drive additional test-set tuning.

Source: the repository's insurance.csv, linked in the original project to Kaggle healthcare-insurance. Original collection and redistribution terms require verification.

Error analysis and subgroup coverage¶

Signed errors are prediction minus actual. Large negative errors are underestimates. Group metrics can be unstable with small counts and do not constitute a complete fairness assessment.

Which inputs does the fitted model rely on?¶

Permutation importance measures the increase in test MAE after shuffling one original feature. Correlated inputs can share importance. This is model reliance, not a causal effect, and is not used for further model selection.

Takeaways¶

The executed tables determine the selected model and results. A separate median baseline establishes whether modeling adds predictive value. Wide calibrated ranges and large individual errors matter even when average performance looks good. Test coverage is empirical, not a guarantee for every subgroup or a future population.

The source has no verified collection dates or current population validity. The original report and notebook are retained as historical coursework; use the new pipeline and reports for current results. Next research steps include provenance verification, external validation, repeated validation of candidate stability, and stronger assessment of group-level error. Do not tune repeatedly against this final test set.
